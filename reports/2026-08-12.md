## 1. Studies / Research

**Mycelium-Based Nonwoven Fabrics via Submerged Fermentation** — *ACS Applied Bio Materials*, published/reported ~2026-08-06 (via phys.org, "Your next handbag could start with mushrooms").
Researchers (Pauliina Ahokas et al.) grew the fungus *Trichoderma reesei* submerged in nutrient-rich liquid inside bioreactor tanks (similar to beer brewing), rather than using traditional solid-substrate mycelium growth. The harvested pulp was combined with sorbitol and cellulose for flexibility/strength, then formed into thin sheets to create a nonwoven, leather-like fabric. Tensile strength in lab tests was comparable to traditional leather; researchers sewed a prototype purse from the material. The authors describe this as solving "a significant bottleneck" for large-scale, affordable production of mycelium-based fabrics.

## 4. Global Developments

**Self-Renewing Living Mycelium Textile** — reported by *Dezeen*, 2026-08-05.
Researchers in China developed a textile made from living mycelium that is self-cleaning and near self-repairing, and can be tinted or made UV-protective via "plug-and-play" add-ons of different fungi or yeast strains. In collaboration with material-innovation company Peelshere, the team produced a prototype dress demonstrating the material's self-renewing, environmentally responsive properties. The development points toward future integration of living biomaterials into architecture and clothing — relevant to circular-economy material systems (biodegradable, low-input alternatives to synthetic textiles) and long-term environmental-engineering applications.
